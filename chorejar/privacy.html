ChoreJar: Privacy Policy

Effective 21 November 2026 · Publisher: Compora Pte. Ltd., Singapore

The short version: ChoreJar keeps one private database for each family. That way both parents, a helper and a child's iPad all see the same week. It holds what you put into the app: names, chores, ticks and the pocket-money records. Grown-ups sign in with Apple, Google or an email link. A child's device joins with a family code and has no account, name or email. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and nothing is sold. You can delete your account, or the whole family, from inside the app.

This policy covers the ChoreJar app for iPhone, iPad and Android, published by Compora Pte. Ltd. ("we", "us") under the Plainsail Apps name. Most of our apps keep everything on your device. ChoreJar is different: a family shares its records between devices, so they are kept online. This page says exactly what is kept, where, and who can see it.

What is kept for your family

The grown-ups' sign-ins. When a parent or helper signs in, our sign-in service keeps:

  • a user ID;
  • the email address that came with the sign-in;
  • the sign-in method (Apple, Google or email link);
  • when the account was created and last used.

With Sign in with Apple you can hide your email, in which case we only see Apple's relay address. We never see or keep a password.

The family's records. The family's database keeps:

  • the family's name;
  • each member's name (as they asked to be called) and role: parent, helper or a child's device;
  • the children's names, as a parent types them;
  • the chores, the pay rules and amounts, each tick and who made it, bonuses and house rules with the reasons you type;
  • passbook entries with their notes, balances, savings goals, the family goal and settings such as currency, time zone and week start;
  • family codes made to invite someone. Each works once and stops working after 7 days.

Don't put anything in a note that you wouldn't want the rest of your family's grown-ups to read.

A child's device. When an iPad or tablet joins with a family code, it gets an anonymous sign-in: a random ID with no name, email address or other details. It is linked to one child, and the family's records note it as "iPad" or "Child's device".

What stays on the device

The device keeps a few things on itself:

  • your sign-in, so you don't have to sign in each time;
  • a few small reminders, such as whether a payday message has been shown;
  • your email address, from asking for an email sign-in link until you open that link.

The camera is used only to scan a family code when you set up a child's device. No picture is saved or sent anywhere.

If you download a passbook, the file is made on your device and goes wherever you choose to send it.

What we do not collect

We collect:

  • no analytics, advertising or tracking tools, and no outside crash-reporting service;
  • no location, contacts, photos or advertising identifiers;
  • no payment details.

ChoreJar does not hold or move money. It is a record of what a family has agreed, and you hand over the money however you like. We do not track you or your children across other companies' apps or websites. We do not sell your information or share it with advertisers or data brokers.

Who can see what

  • Parents see and change everything in their own family.
  • A helper can tick chores. They see goals as a percentage, never amounts, balances or the passbook.
  • A child's device shows that child's own week, and can't change anything. It shows their savings only if a parent turns on "Show savings".
  • Other families can't see anything of yours. Every request is checked against your family's membership by the database's security rules.

As the app's operator, we can technically reach the database. We look at a family's records only to fix a problem you have asked us about, or when the law requires it.

Where the data is held

  • Compora Pte. Ltd. publishes and operates the app, in Singapore.
  • Google (Firebase) holds the family database (Cloud Firestore) in Singapore. It also runs the sign-in service and sends the email sign-in links, on its worldwide infrastructure. Google acts on our behalf under its Firebase terms. To provide the service, it processes network information such as your device's IP address.
  • Apple and Google handle Sign in with Apple and Sign in with Google under their own privacy policies. They tell us only who signed in.

How long we keep it

Your family's records are kept for as long as the family uses ChoreJar. A deletion takes effect straight away. We keep no backups of our own, so deleted records can't be recovered.

Your choices

  • Delete your account in Account › Delete › Delete my account. You leave the family and your sign-in is deleted. Ticks and entries you made stay in the family's records.
  • Delete the whole family. A parent can do this, and it's offered to the last parent when they delete their account. Every record of the family is deleted, then that parent's sign-in. It can't be undone.
  • Remove someone. In Account › Family members, a parent can remove the helper, the other parent or a child's device.
  • Deleting the app clears what is on that device. It does not delete the family's records, because the rest of the family still uses them. Use the options above for that.
  • Ask us. Email support@plainsailapps.com from the address you sign in with. We'll tell you what is kept for your family, correct it or delete it.

Children

ChoreJar is made for parents to run. Grown-ups tick the chores after asking the child, and children see only a look-only screen. Their names are typed in by a parent. A child's device has no account, email address or other details, and can't change anything. We do not knowingly collect personal information directly from children. A parent can see, change or delete everything about their children at any time, in the app or by emailing us.

Changes
